01 / ORIGIN

It was never
something I
discovered.

I was born in Santa Tereza, Belo Horizonte — a neighbourhood that has always felt more like a small town inside the city.

My grandparents were already living there long before I was born. My mother grew up there. My grandfather and my uncles all played music, and some of my earliest memories are of long family gatherings built around songs, instruments and people playing together.

My grandmother was a friend of Maricota Borges, mother of Lô and Márcio Borges. The music that became known as Clube da Esquina was simply part of the world around me.

Milton Nascimento, Lô Borges, Beto Guedes, Toninho Horta, Tavinho Moura — their harmonies, accents, expressions and ways of being have always sounded familiar. They remind me of people from home.

The music of Minas feels less like an influence than a language I grew up speaking.

SANTÊ

the affectionate name for Santa Tereza

02 / RECORDING

2022 · original composition

Santê

feat. Toninho Horta · Esdra Ferreira “Neném” · Enéias Xavier

A piece about walking through the streets where everything began.

Santê is what we call Santa Tereza. I wrote the piece trying to translate the feeling of walking through those streets — a place inside a large capital city that still carries the intimacy of a small town.

The harmony comes from the musical language that surrounded me from childhood. Having Toninho Horta on the recording gave the piece another layer of meaning: someone whose sound had been part of that landscape for as long as I could remember was now playing on a composition about the neighbourhood itself.

03 / FORMATION
Music education
was never confined
to the classroom.

After five years living in Porto Alegre and becoming involved with its local music scene, I returned to Minas Gerais. In 2014 I joined Bituca — Universidade de Música Popular.

There I studied with musicians including Ian Guest, Gilvan de Oliveira, Lincoln Cheib and Enéias Xavier.

Years inside Belo Horizonte’s music scene also brought friendships, work and encounters with Toninho Horta, Juarez Moreira, Lô Borges, Beto Guedes, Gilvan de Oliveira, Gabriel Guedes, Rodrigo Borges, Enéias Xavier, Magno Alexandre and Robertinho Brant. I was particularly close to the late Vander Lee, and also had the opportunity to know Fernando Brant and Milton Nascimento.

My parallel work in photography and design often crossed paths with the same community, making music, friendship and visual work different parts of the same life.

04 / INTERPRETATION

2025 · composition by Edelson Pantera

Faial

Deangelo Silva · Felipe Continentino · Igor Gosling

A composition I wanted to inhabit, not simply reproduce.

Faial was written by Edelson Pantera, a musician and poet whose work I deeply admire.

For the recording, I wanted musicians with unmistakable voices. My brother Igor Gosling joined on bass. Felipe Continentino brought a completely distinctive approach to the drums — his performance feels almost like a composition within the composition itself.

On piano, Deangelo Silva brought the harmonic imagination that makes him such a remarkable musician. He also performs with Hamilton de Holanda, who was my patron at my Bituca graduation.

05 / COLLABORATION
CANÇÃO DA ESTRADA ABERTA

Daniel Gosling · Mário Wamser · lyrics by Murilo Antunes

It began with harmony.

I had written a harmonic sequence without a melody or title. The initial inspiration came from the movement of Trem Azul: major seventh chords drifting into unexpected places, modal interchange, and the feeling that the harmony itself is travelling.

I asked my friend Mário Wamser to imagine a melody around it. He returned with a beautiful melody — and an entirely new B section.

We then showed the piece to Murilo Antunes, one of the historic lyricists of Clube da Esquina. He immediately agreed to write for it.

Murilo returned with Canção da Estrada Aberta, inspired by Walt Whitman’s Song of the Open Road.

A piece that began as a sequence of wandering chords suddenly had its destination.

06 / MINAS → AUSTRALIA

Fourteen thousand
kilometres away,
the songs found
a new room.

Now based in Adelaide, I continue to perform and record Brazilian music while carrying a particularly strong connection to the music of Minas Gerais.

In 2023 I performed at Nexus Arts as part of Interplay during Adelaide Fringe, presenting a concert dedicated to classics from Clube da Esquina and Minas Gerais.

For me, it was less about recreating a sound from Brazil than allowing a musical language I had carried all my life to exist naturally in a new place.

08 / SHORT BIO

Daniel Gosling

Daniel Gosling is a Brazilian guitarist and composer from Santa Tereza, Belo Horizonte, whose music is deeply rooted in the musical language of Minas Gerais.

Born into a musical family in the neighbourhood where much of the Clube da Esquina story began, Gosling grew up surrounded by its music and by personal connections to the community behind it.

After five years in Porto Alegre, he returned to Minas Gerais and joined Bituca — Universidade de Música Popular in 2014. His recordings continue that conversation rather than trying to reproduce it: Santê, featuring Toninho Horta; Faial, composed by Edelson Pantera; and Canção da Estrada Aberta, created with Mário Wamser and lyricist Murilo Antunes.

Now based in Adelaide, Australia, Gosling continues to build a bridge between the music of Minas Gerais and a new musical landscape.
