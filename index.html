01 / SANTA TEREZA

Before it was
music history,
it was home.

Daniel grew up in Santa Tereza, Belo Horizonte — the neighbourhood at the heart of Clube da Esquina.

Music was already in the family. And the songs of Milton, Lô, Beto and Toninho were never distant references; they belonged to the same streets, accents and atmosphere.

Not an influence discovered later. A language learned early.

SANTÊ

what Santa Tereza feels like when you belong there

02 / SANTÊ

Original composition

Santê

feat. Toninho Horta · Esdra Ferreira “Neném” · Enéias Xavier

A neighbourhood turned into music.

Santê is Daniel's portrait of Santa Tereza. Familiar streets, Minas harmony and the intimacy of a place that shaped a generation of Brazilian music.

Then Toninho Horta stepped into the recording — one of the sounds that had always belonged to that landscape.

03 / THE CIRCLE
The records became
teachers. The teachers
became friends.

After years in Porto Alegre, Daniel returned to Minas and studied at Bituca, with musicians including Ian Guest, Gilvan de Oliveira, Lincoln Cheib and Enéias Xavier.

But the real education was larger: rehearsals, stages, conversations and friendships across Belo Horizonte's music scene — a living culture rather than a museum piece.

04 / FAIAL

Composition by Edelson Pantera

Faial

Deangelo Silva · Felipe Continentino · Igor Gosling

Four musicians. No interest in playing it safe.

Faial begins with a composition Daniel loved enough not to leave untouched.

With Deangelo Silva, Felipe Continentino and Igor Gosling, the recording became less a cover than a conversation — elastic, rhythmic and alive.

05 / OPEN ROAD
CANÇÃO DAESTRADAABERTA

Daniel Gosling · Mário Wamser · lyrics by Murilo Antunes

It started with a handful of chords looking for somewhere to go.

Mário Wamser found the melody. Murilo Antunes — one of Clube da Esquina's historic lyricists — found the words, inspired by Walt Whitman's Song of the Open Road.

The harmony was wandering. The song gave it a road.
